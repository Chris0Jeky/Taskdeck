# Market and Value Notes (Practical)

## What users “buy”
They do not buy “a Kanban board.”
They buy:
- reduced cognitive load
- reduced fear of forgetting
- reduced context-switch cost
- reduced guilt from missed tasks
- confidence that the system is keeping track

Your product promise is: **less overhead than the alternatives**.

## The market reality
There are two broad groups of competitors:

### A) Work management boards (Trello/Notion/Asana/Linear-like)
Strengths:
- polished UI
- established workflows
Weaknesses:
- admin overhead and “paperwork”
- many are SaaS-first (privacy concerns, lock-in, subscriptions)

### B) Meeting/transcript-to-task tools (Otter/Fireflies/Teams/Copilot-like)
Strengths:
- auto-extract action items
- integrations with meetings
Weaknesses:
- often not “your system of record”
- data privacy sensitivity
- extraction can be noisy / hallucinations → trust problems

## Your wedge
You’re combining:
- board as system of record
- intake automation
- trust model (proposal-first + provenance)
- local-first

This is credible because you already built proposal-first automation infrastructure.

## Value proposition wording (draft)
“When you dump messy notes, Taskdeck turns them into structured tasks and safely updates your board via reviewable proposals — without you doing the admin work.”

## Monetization direction (don’t implement now; keep it in mind)
Two directions that fit local-first:
- Self-hosted “Pro” (license or subscription) with premium packs + advanced automation + optional cloud sync later
- Open-core + paid add-ons (sync, SSO, enterprise controls) later

For now: your priority is retention and polish, not pricing.
